Indicative Particulate Matter Categories (CPCB Breakpoints)
CPCB concentration breakpoints are applied here to classify instantaneous simulated PM2.5 and PM10 observations. Note: These values represent indicative pollutant categories and do not constitute an official composite 24-hour CPCB NAQI score.
  • Good PM2.5 ≤ 30 | PM10 ≤ 50 µg/m³ (Minimal health impact)
  • Satisfactory PM2.5 ≤ 60 | PM10 ≤ 100 µg/m³ (Minor breathing discomfort to sensitive people)
  • Moderate PM2.5 ≤ 90 | PM10 ≤ 250 µg/m³ (Discomfort to asthmatics and elderly)
  • Poor PM2.5 ≤ 120 | PM10 ≤ 350 µg/m³ (Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure)
  • Very Poor PM2.5 ≤ 250 | PM10 ≤ 430 µg/m³ (Respiratory illness on prolonged exposure)
  • Severe PM2.5 > 250 | PM10 > 430 µg/m³ (Severe health impact on all populations)
Public Health Advisory

Particulate matter emissions in Bengaluru vary significantly by urban morphology: industrial transport hubs (ST004) and high-density tech corridors (ST002) show elevated coarse and fine dust concentrations compared to leafy residential sectors (ST003).

Sensitive groups should restrict strenuous outdoor activities when station PM2.5 concentrations exceed 60 µg/m³.

Calculation Governance & Guards
Heat index values are generated via the NOAA National Weather Service (NWS) Rothfusz regression model and Steadman preliminary formulation.
  • Low-Temp Policy: Suppressed below 20°C (68°F); returns NOT_APPLICABLE_LOW_TEMPERATURE.
  • Conservative Guard: Conditions exceeding T > 110°F and RH > 40% are halted to prevent unphysical polynomial divergence (UNSUPPORTED_EXTRAPOLATION).
  • Legacy Alerts: Historical alerts missing provenance metadata are classified as LEGACY_UNVERIFIED.
